Why a translator should weigh less than its dictionary

Most desktop translators either bundle a whole browser or lock the good engines behind a monthly plan. The trick is to lean on what the system already ships: the webview, the clipboard, the speech voices — and only reach for the network when a sentence actually needs translating.

That constraint shapes everything downstream. A popup that appears in under 150 ms cannot wait for a runtime to boot, so the window is created once and simply shown. A clipboard that must be returned untouched cannot be used as scratch space for longer than a few hundred milliseconds.

None of this is novel. It is the same discipline that made the small utilities of the early 2010s feel instant, applied to a stack that finally makes them pleasant to look at.
